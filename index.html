I am an undergraduate researcher at Berkeley AI Research, advised by Prof. Dawn Song. My work is on diffusion language models: how they generate text, what their hidden states reveal about the dependencies between tokens, and how to find the prompts that make them fail.

Most of my projects come back to the same question, which is how to invert a generative model. Given a response, can we recover the prompt that produced it? Given a model's hidden states, can we recover the dependency structure it learned? Given one modality of biological data, can we recover another? Previously I worked at Databricks, at Palo Alto Networks, where I filed two patents on LLM security, and at IBM Research. I also co-founded a stealth startup building foundation models for biological data.

  • Diffusion Investigator Agents

    Jai Sharma, Zhanhui Zhou, Dawn Song

    Preprint, 2026

    Reverse generation is the process of finding a prompt that maximizes the probability of a specific target response, and it is one of the main strategies for red-teaming a language model. Autoregressive models struggle with it because they generate sequentially. Diffusion language models can generate in arbitrary orders, so we treat the problem as a completion task in which the prompt tokens are masked. Applying Bayes' Theorem to the dLLM objective gives a sampling strategy that maximizes the divergence between the conditional and unconditional distributions, which provides a formal link between reverse generation and classifier-free guidance. We extend this into a Dynamic Diffusion Attack that folds a history of successful jailbreaks and failed refusals back into the guidance objective. On harmful string datasets it reaches a 92.86% attack success rate, compared to 65.38% for Greedy Coordinate Gradient.

  • Neural Estimation of Pairwise Mutual Information in Masked Discrete Sequence Models

    Jai Sharma*, Yifan Wang*, Bryan Li*

    Preprint, 2025  ·  arXiv:2605.20187

    Masked diffusion models expose marginal conditional distributions, but they do not explicitly represent which variables depend on each other. We estimate the full pairwise mutual information matrix directly from the hidden states of a pretrained model, supervised by ground-truth MI computed from the model's own conditional distributions. The estimator predicts the whole matrix in a single forward pass, which lets us identify conditionally independent subsets of variables and decode them in parallel. On Sudoku and on protein sequence generation with ESM-C, the MI maps recover known structural constraints and reduce inference-time forward passes by 3 to 5 times while preserving generative quality and outperforming entropy-based parallelization.

  • RNA-seq characterization of histamine-releasing mast cells as potential therapeutic target of osteoarthritis

    Xiaoyi Zhao, Shady Younis, Hui Shi, et al. Robinson Lab, Stanford School of Medicine

    Clinical Immunology (Elsevier), vol. 244, 2022

    This study examines the role of mast cells in osteoarthritis. Single-cell and bulk RNA-Seq of OA synovial tissue identify prototypical mast cell markers, and a marker expression score classifies 40 patients into mast cell high, medium, and low synovial pathotypes. Treating mice with a histamine H1 receptor antagonist attenuates disease. I conducted the biostatistical analysis of the mRNA-seq data, which covered over 40,000 genes and 1,000 samples.

Writing

  • Diffusion from First Principles

    Book, in progress, 2025

    The purpose of this text is to introduce diffusion in machine learning from first principles. Diffusion is a physical term before it is a machine learning one, so the book builds up the forward SDE, the Fokker-Planck equation, the reverse SDE, the Boltzmann distribution, the Einstein-Stokes relation, Langevin sampling, and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.

Building

  • Stealth

    Stealth startup

    Co-founder and Chief AI Officer, NVIDIA Inception, 2024 to 2025

    I co-founded a startup building foundation models for multimodal biological data. We trained several autoencoders simultaneously, one per modality, each minimizing its own reconstruction loss while also minimizing the distance between the embeddings of the same patient across modalities. Cross-modality cosine similarity exceeded 0.99. Because the modalities share a latent space, we could translate one into another. At inference time, using only mRNA data (2.0% of all raw features) retained 92% of model performance, using only methylation (2.9%) retained 85%, and using only microRNA (0.3%) retained 88%. We also ran a pilot on reverse engineering the probability of technical success in clinical trials, and our findings were validated by domain experts.
