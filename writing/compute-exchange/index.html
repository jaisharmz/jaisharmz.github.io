A Provider’s Perspective of a Compute Exchange

AI compute is scarce and intrinsically valuable, but a lack of standardization and divisibility keeps it from being traded the way oil and gold are. This white paper models GPU resource allocation from a provider’s point of view: pricing an ask against competing bids, the cost of sampling utilization slowly, energy and compute prices as coupled stochastic processes, and a correlation metric for a tokens market.

3Example: Modeling GPU Price with Gamma Distributions

Consider a Poisson process for the incoming bids for compute P(λ)P(\lambda)P(λ). A Gamma distribution Γ(k,λ)\Gamma (k, \lambda)Γ(k,λ) can help us model multiple incoming bids, or competitors, for the compute. Therefore, the profit can be modeled as: E[Profit]=(B−C)⋅P(B<B∗)E[\text{Profit}] = (B - C)\cdot P(B<B^*)E[Profit]=(B−C)⋅P(B<B∗) Here, BBB is the ask from the provider, B∗B^*B∗ is the lowest ask from any competitor provider, and CCC is the marginal cost of a GPU. This can be seen as the product of marginal profit of winning the bid weighted by the probability of winning the bid, or being lower than the lowest ask. The goal of the provider is to set BBB to maximize the expected profit.

If ρ\rhoρ is convex, compute prices are stable when energy is cheap. However, once energy hits a certain threshold, compute prices become hyper volatile. If κ\kappaκ is high, mean reversion is fast and providers can afford to have lower sampling rates for resource utilization. However, if κ\kappaκ is small, the discretization error is wrong for longer.

The profit is given by the difference between compute price and energy cost: Profitt=Ct−Et\text{Profit}_t = C_t - E_tProfitt​=Ct​−Et​

Here, we make a small note about the correlation between dW1,tdW_{1,t}dW1,t​ and dW2,tdW_{2,t}dW2,t​. If high, when energy spikes, the price of compute follows immediately. Therefore, the provider has a natural hedge, since they can pass the energy spike onto the customer faster. However, if low, the compute price might not be as reactive to the spikes in energy cost. This can cause the Value at Risk (VaR) to increase exponentially.

6Correlation Metric for Tokens Market

This section is a small remark about an information-theoretic metric for correlation of tokens in a decentralized compute tokens market.
